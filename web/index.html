      For the stats nerds
      Model
      Calibration

      These two settings change every number on the site, the match centre included. Features: rolling goals, shots on target, possession and form over the last five games, head to head, home advantage and an Elo rating, all from before kickoff.

      Log loss against the obvious alternatives

      Log loss punishes confident wrong calls, lower is better. Always home means guessing a home win every time with the usual frequencies as probabilities. The bookmakers are the average closing odds with their margin stripped out.

      What it predicted, what happened

      Rows are the real result, columns the model's pick. The diagonal is where it got it right.

      When it says 40%, is it 40%?

      Every predicted probability, bucketed, against how often that outcome actually happened. On the dotted line the probabilities can be taken at face value.

      What it leans on

      Permutation importance: shuffle one input across the test season and see how much worse the log loss gets.

      About draws

      Around a quarter of games end level, but a draw is almost never the single most likely result, so a model that only picks the favourite never calls one. Training with balanced class weights (the Raw setting) makes it call draws, and it catches a fair few, but it's wrong more often overall and its percentages run too high on draws. Calibration pulls the percentages back to reality, and in doing so it stops calling draws altogether. Flip between them above and watch the tiles.

      The leakage tests, the Elo details and the full results table are in the README on GitHub.
